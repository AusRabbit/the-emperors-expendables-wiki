The collars every member of the crew wears, fitted before they were handed to [[cassian-vorn]]. What they really are came out on [[vannix-station]], from [[telsa-brie]]:

*"Cresh Spike is the restraint system. Collars, implants, remote compliance hardware. Batch-linked. Military prison stock, ISB-modified. It can stun you, drop you, track you, maybe kill you if the calibration is cruel enough."*

## What They Do

Tracking, forced compliance, remote stun, and a sealed "final response". The collars are batch-synced to a remote command node. A station like Vannix can mirror and route their traffic, but cannot remove or control them.

Cutting the wrong line makes a collar think its wearer is escaping custody. [[telsa-brie]]'s one diagnostic on [[vesh-talorr]]'s collar threw a blue-white arc and slammed him into a wall.

## The Leads

- With a calibration station, or a live technician console and officer-level keys, it might be possible to spoof a maintenance cycle — perhaps enough to disable the remote stun. *"Maybe."*
- A maintenance handshake can forge a limited status update or interfere with future synchronisation. It cannot take a collar off.
- [[kenno-thorn]], during the fitting: a magnetic latch, a receiver node at the nape, and a recessed technician's service port.
- [[vesh-talorr]]: a batch-synchronisation window in how the collars are handled.

## The Relay

The command relay during Mission 01 was the gunship [[black-warrant]], whose trace [[telsa-brie]] looped. It made no difference: at the threshold of [[bay-nine]], the final response dropped all five collar-bearers at once, independent of any local trace, console or the gunship. [[cassian-vorn]] holds the authority behind it, and carries a collar-control cylinder.

Where the root controller is, and whether the final response can be blocked or reversed, are still [[threads|open questions]].
